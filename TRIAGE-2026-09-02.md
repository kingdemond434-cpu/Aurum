## The headline is wrong

**"Aurum is unprofitable" is not yet a measurement.** The number behind it is
`edge: -0.125R per trade over 37 resolved (15 wins)`. Three reasons it cannot carry that claim:

- **n = 37.** At a typical R-distribution, the standard error on mean R over 37 trades is roughly
  ±0.16R. −0.125R sits comfortably inside noise. The desk's own gate requires **30 resolved trades
  before a deflated Sharpe is even computed**, and 37 barely clears its own floor.
- **`ledger integrity: 26 duplicate decision_id(s)`.** Some fraction of those 37 may be the same
  decision counted twice. Until the duplicates are resolved, the denominator is unknown.
- **`spread profile: absent — every expectancy figure is priced against the FEED's spread, not the
  venue that actually fills you`.** And the venue check is failing because the attached terminal is
  **Fusion Markets, not Vantage**. So the cost model is measured against the wrong broker.

−0.125R over 37 trades with duplicate rows and the wrong spread model is not a verdict on the
strategy. It is a reading taken with a broken instrument. **Do not rebuild the architecture on it.**

## Changes made

### Signals now come from the subscription, with Codex as the fallback

Default primary is `claudecode:claude-opus-5` (`run_desk.py`, `golddesk/service.py`,
`deploy/aurum-desk.service`); `codex:gpt-5.6-sol` stays as the fallback.

**The bug behind the 1,030 blind bars.** `build_service` read:

```python
fallbacks = () if primary_name in {"deterministic", "replay", "codex"} else ...
```

A **codex primary was handed an empty chain**. Every non-zero CLI exit became a bar the desk
never read rather than a bar answered by someone else. `codex` is out of that set now, and the
chain also drops any fallback matching the primary — otherwise `--provider codex` picks up the
default codex fallback and retries the same broken CLI, reporting a failover that changed nothing.
`deterministic` and `replay` still refuse failover, because a replay that silently substitutes a
live model is not a replay.

### Gold only

`golddesk/intake.py` filters candidate cells to gold symbols (`XAU*`, `GC`, `MGC`, `GOLD`) and
**retires the foreign cells already in the book**. Filtering intake alone would have left the 77
already registered accruing days forever.

This is a gate *loosened*, not tightened. A registered cell occupies a shadow slot, accrues forward
days, and counts as a concurrent test in the book-level promotion correction — so ~70 FX cells were
raising the bar every gold cell had to clear. Dropping them makes the gold book **easier** to
promote from.

### Wakes on four arms instead of one

`timeframes` defaults to `("M5", "M15", "H1", "H4")`. Each wakes on its own close: roughly
288 + 96 + 24 + 6 evaluation points per day against 96 before.

**Two costs to watch.** It multiplies analyst calls ~4.3×, and it multiplies the trial count the
promotion gate deflates for. Also: `claudecode` reads take ~78s against ~8s for the metered API.
That fits inside a 300s M5 bar, but at an H4 close all four arms fire together — ~5 minutes of
sequential reads against the next M5 close. If that proves tight, the M5 arm is the one to drop
first, not the provider.

## What I did not do, and why

**I did not remove the compiler or model refusal gates.** The state file shows `REFUSAL_COMPILER 93`
and `REFUSAL_MODEL 114` against `SIGNAL 40` — 38% of decisions refused by the compiler alone.

That rate is a **bug signal, not a strictness signal**. The compiler refuses on money-path
geometry: stop the wrong side of entry, target the wrong side, buffer inconsistent with the
structural level. Those refusals are the only thing standing between a malformed read and a signal
sent to you with an inverted stop. Disabling them would raise signal count immediately and would
raise it with broken signals.

The right move is to read *why* those 93 refused — if one geometry rule is firing on most of them,
that is a fixable defect that returns those signals legitimately. Loosening the rule returns them
whether or not they were ever valid. I would rather find the defect than delete the check, and the
refusal ledger already carries the reason codes needed to do it.

Same logic for the multiplicity haircut: `dsr_deflated: null` with `n_trials_searched: 3168` is not
a harsh gate, it is a gate that **is not running**. Turning it on is what lets the gold cells be
judged on 3,168 trials instead of pretending there was one.
